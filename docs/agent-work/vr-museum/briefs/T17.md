## Goal and non-goals

A. **Preview from here.** The Preview button always starts at the spawn point, so testing a room means walking to it. Add a viewer
start position in the URL and an editor button that opens the viewer at the selected item or the current view.

B. **Check museum.** A button that lists what would go wrong for visitors: rooms and audio that cannot be reached, doorways too

item shows it on the blueprint.

`npm run seal`, no change to `museum.json` or the schema, no change to collision or locomotion behaviour, no change to how a plaque looks.

### Walking model: match the viewer exactly
`resolveMove` stops the head at distance `R = thickness / 2 + PLAYER_RADIUS` from every wall SEGMENT (centre line, without the drawn T/2
extensions). So a straight gap of width g between wall ends is walkable only when `g > 2R`, which is `T + 0.5`, 0.70 m at the default thickness. The Door tool
allows 0.5 m, so this occurs in practice. Teleporting only needs the line to clear each segment by T/2 and the target to clear by R, so teleport can pass a gap too narrow to walk. Report against
walking, the only way to move on desktop, and say in the message that teleporting in VR may still get through.

### 1. Narrow doorways (exact, not from the grid)
For every pair of wall ends of different walls that are on one line (parallel within about 1 degree and within 1 cm off each other's line), with the walls running away from the gap in opposite
directions: gap `g`. It is a too-narrow doorway when `T < g < 2R` and no other wall covers the gap's midpoint (distance from midpoint to any other wall's segment `<= T / 2` means covered: this
is the case of two rooms drawn side by side with overlapping walls). At or below T the drawn wall ends touch, so it is not a doorway: skip it. Severity `warning`, target the wall, `at` the midpoint, for example:
`Doorway is 0.50 m wide: too narrow to walk through (needs more than 0.70 m). Teleporting in VR may still get through.`

### 2. Reachability (grid flood fill)
- Grid over `computeFloorBounds(m, 2)`. Cell size 0.05 m, or larger when that would exceed 20 million cells (`cell = max(0.05, sqrt(area / 20e6))`).
- A cell is blocked when its centre is within `R - slack` of a wall segment, with `slack = 0.8 * cell`. The slack exists because a free band narrower than `sqrt(2) * cell` can
  fall between cell centres and would break a real passage: never make a walkable corridor look closed. Rasterise per wall (only cells in the wall's bounding box grown by R), not cells times walls.
- Every too-narrow doorway from step 1 gets a blocking segment across its gap in this grid, so slack cannot leak through it.
- Flood fill with 4 neighbours from `pushOut(spawn)`, or the nearest free cell. If no free cell exists within 1 m: `problem`, `The start point has no room to stand`, and skip the reachability items.
- Label the remaining free cells into components (needed for grouping below).
- Viewing a work: a work counts as reachable when a reached cell lies on its normal within 2 m of the wall face with no other wall crossing the line from the face to that cell (`segmentSegmentDistance` `<= T / 2` means crossing).
  Works that fail are grouped by the component of the first free cell along their normal, or as "nowhere to stand" when there is none: `problem`, one item per group, `at` the group's centre, target the first work.
  For example `3 paintings cannot be reached from the start point: "A", "B", "C"` (cap the list at 5 names with "and n more"), or `"A" has no room in front of it`.
- Zones: a zone with no reached cell inside its shape: `problem`, target the zone, for example `Audio zone "X" cannot be heard: no visitor can walk into it`.
- Leaving the building: if the fill reaches the outer edge of the grid: `warning`, no target, `Visitors can walk out of the building through a gap in the outer walls. The floor ends 2 m past the walls, but walking does not stop there.`
  Note that the outside then connects everything, so that is why this one matters. An open-air garden makes this a false alarm, and it is only a warning.
- Empty sealed rooms: no item at all.

### 3. Labels (only for works whose media `hasLabel`, using `labelLayout`)
Using the placement above, the plaque covers `[l0, l1]` along the wall. All with 1 cm tolerance.
- Past the wall run: the wall's run is the union of drawn ranges (`wallSpans`) of walls on the same line (same tolerances as in step 1; a wall drawn on top of it counts). When `[l0, l1]` is not entirely inside
  that union: `warning`, target the work, `Label of "X" sticks out past the wall by 0.12 m`, measured to the nearest edge of the union. That includes a doorway and a free wall end.
- Into a wall: the segment `[l0, l1]` on the work's face plane, offset 1 cm outward, comes within `T / 2` of a wall that is not on the same line, and that wall lies on the work's side: `warning`, `Label of "X" runs into a wall`. A
  wall that only meets the work's wall on the other side must not trigger this: test it. The wall's own height is not considered.
