## Goal and non-goals
The museum has flat hemisphere + ambient light and no shadows, so walls, floors and paintings look like they float. Add
faked, static soft shadows where surfaces meet, drawn as thin strips with an alpha gradient, all in ONE merged mesh so it
costs one extra draw call and no textures. It must suit a Meta Quest.

Non-goals: no shadow maps, no real-time or moving lights, no new textures or render targets, no `museum.json` or schema
change, no editor UI or museum setting, no changes to materials on walls/floors/ceilings, no strips on door headers or
ceiling step quads, no change to paintings' streaming.

## Done when
- With shadows on, the viewer shows: a darkening at the foot of every drawn wall face, on the wall and on the floor; the
  same at the top of a wall face where it meets a ceiling, on the wall and on the ceiling; a soft drop shadow around every
  painting frame, slightly lower than the frame.
- No ceiling strips where there is no ceiling: `settings.ceiling` false, or under open sky.
- Ceiling strips sit at the height of the room on THAT side of the wall, also where a wall changes height along its
  length or the two sides differ.
- A floor strip never ends in a hard cut across its width where a wall has a free end (a doorway jamb) or at an exposed
  convex corner: the shadow wraps around, per "Ends and corners" below.
- All walls, any angle (not only axis-aligned), whichever direction a to b runs.
- Exactly one extra mesh for everything above, and no extra materials or textures elsewhere. `?debug` draw calls go up by
  exactly 1 compared with `?shadows=0` on the same view.
- `?shadows=0` on the viewer URL builds the scene without the mesh (for comparing, and for measuring on a Quest).
- An empty museum (no walls, no works) does not crash and adds no mesh.
- No z-fighting or shimmer at any distance, including over stacked floor areas.
- All tests and the build pass, and renders exist (see Verification).

## Context and exact contracts

### New file: src/viewer/contactShadows.ts
```ts
export interface ContactShadowInput {
  museum: Museum;
  /** Result of wallPieces(...) in scene.ts: the drawn pieces per wall id. */
  pieces: Map<string, WallPiece[]>;
  /** Result of wallEnds(...) in scene.ts. */
  ends: Map<string, { a: WallEnd; b: WallEnd }>;
  /** decomposeCeiling(...).groups, or [] when settings.ceiling is false. */
  ceilings: readonly CeilingGroup[];
  /** The works as placed by scene.ts. */
  slots: readonly PaintingSlot[];
}
/** World-space geometry with `position` (3) and `color` (itemSize 4: rgb 0, a = strength). Null when empty. */
export function contactShadowGeometry(input: ContactShadowInput): THREE.BufferGeometry | null;
export function contactShadowMaterial(): THREE.Material;
```
Import `PaintingSlot` as a type only, to avoid a cycle with scene.ts. The tuning values live as named constants at the top of
this file: strip widths, peak alpha per kind, frame shadow offset. Reasonable starting points, tune by eye from the renders:
wall foot and floor strip 0.35 m, ceiling contact 0.3 m, peak alpha about 0.2, frame shadow reach about 5 cm, shifted down about 1.5 cm.

Use three rows across a strip (peak, about a third of peak in the middle, zero) rather than two, because a straight linear ramp
shows a visible edge. The peak row sits against the joint.

Material: `MeshBasicMaterial` with `vertexColors: true, transparent: true, depthWrite: false, fog: false, side: FrontSide`,
plus `polygonOffset: true` with negative factor and units so it wins against the surface it lies on at every distance. See
the comment by the frames in scene.ts for why a depth bias, not only a physical gap, is what prevents flicker here. three.js
uses the alpha channel only when the `color` attribute has itemSize 4: confirm this in a render, not only in a test. Winding
must face the visible side of each surface.

### Positions
Each strip lies a small physical distance off its surface (1 to 2 mm) as well as having the depth bias:
- Wall strips: on each face plane at `wall line + normal * (T/2 + gap)`, along each drawn piece `[from, to]` (metres along the
  wall from `a`; `from` may be negative, `to` may pass the length because of end extensions). Foot strip height is limited to
  the piece's `top`.
- Floor strips: at `y = (highest areaQuads(m).y, or 0) + gap`, because floor areas stack 1 mm per level. Running from the
  face plane outward.
- Ceiling strips: for each `CeilingGroup` rect, clip each face line (the face plane, in plan) against the rect. For the
  clipped interval, draw on the wall face downward from `group.height` and on the ceiling (just below `group.height`, facing
  down) outward. A rect that the face line does not cross gives nothing; because open sky is not in `groups`, it gets no strip.
  Limit the clipping to the drawn pieces, so doorways stay free.
- Frame drop shadow: a ring on the wall around each slot's frame rectangle (`slot.w + 0.04` by `slot.h + 0.04`, position and
  `rotationY` from the slot; plane faces +z), shifted down, alpha peak at the inner edge and zero at the outer edge. It needs
  no centre, since the frame covers it. It must lie behind the frame (the frame's back is flush with the wall, front 1 cm off).

### Ends and corners
- Free wall end (`WallEnd.cap` true, also on a piece with `capFrom`/`capTo`): add a half-disc fan on the floor around the end
  of the wall's footprint, peak alpha at the wall footprint and zero at the rim, matching the strips' profile so no seam
  shows. Also add a foot strip on the cap face itself (the door jamb), so the dark band does not stop at the wall's edge.
- Exposed convex corner (exactly two walls meet at an end, not collinear, seen from the outside of the bend): fill the
  wedge between the two outer floor strips with a fan, same profile. Inner corners get nothing extra: two strips overlapping
  there read correctly as a darker corner. T-junctions get nothing extra: the ends are hidden inside the other wall.
- Pieces cut by overlap handling (`capFrom`/`capTo` false, not a true wall end) get no fan.
Use a modest segment count, 6 to 8 per half-disc.
